The counterfactual

"Before Claude Code" splits two ways

Before language models existed at all, this product cannot be built. It is eight language-model agents in a trenchcoat. The 2019 version of this idea is a decision-tree quiz, or a services business with human stylists on payroll. That is a different company, not a cheaper version of this one.

The answerable question is the second one: with the API available, but every line written by hand. That estimate follows.

Two credible shapes come out of that. A team of five or six working in parallel: 10–14 weeks. A single senior generalist doing all of it: 8–12 months, and realistically a worse product, because one person rarely does store research, brand voice, and streaming transport equally well.

Actual cost: two days, one operator, and API spend in the low hundreds of dollars. Roughly three orders of magnitude on money and about 40× on calendar time.

Where the comparison flatters the machine

A real team's three months would have included things this app has never had. Store visits — the directory is web-researched, and a human would have walked into M Penner and Hamilton Shirts; twelve stores still lack full enrichment and sixteen discovered candidates sit unverified. Real users — nobody has used this yet, and a team's timeline would have included testing that persona sweeps cannot replace. A designer with taste. And disagreement: a team argues about the naming, the voice rules, the front page. Those calls were made here in minutes, and fast is not the same as right.

The field

Who you are actually up against

Ordered by distance from your customer. The nearest threat is not the best-funded one.

Nearest · already selling this in Houston

Houston stylists and rep-based clothiers

Lucho sells wardrobe styling consultation in Houston and has been family-run since 1989. They are a recommendation inside your plans and a company selling your service. Tom James, Q Clothier, and Norton Ditto run on the same model: a person who builds a man's wardrobe over years, and in Tom James's case comes to his office to do it. Independents like By Brayden and JS Personal Styling work the same city, at $120–200 an hour or $150–500 a session.

This is either your best partnership channel or your sharpest conflict, and it is worth deciding which on purpose.

Every one of them sends a shopper to a website. Yours sends a man to a building, with a sentence to say when he walks in. Their scale works against them here: a national player cannot research 43 stores in every city, which is exactly why none of them has.

Crowded · weak moat

Closet and outfit apps

Whering, Indyx, Acloset, Lookastic, Stylebook, DLOOK, Glance, StylistIQ. Mostly free to $20 a month, mostly "photograph your closet, receive outfits." None of them knows Houston, and none of them will name the tailor a man should walk into on Saturday.

The graveyard · read it twice

Styling subscriptions that already failed

Nordstrom shut Trunk Club down in 2022. Stitch Fix revenue fell 16% to $1.3B with subscribers shrinking and its founder returning as CEO. The industry called it box fatigue.

Read that as good news and a warning. You hold no inventory, ship no boxes, and eat no returns, so the economics that killed them do not apply. But investors carry scar tissue, and "AI stylist" will make some of them flinch before you finish the sentence. Lead with the store-referral model, not the styling model.

The gap is not an accident. It is unglamorous work: somebody has to research 43 stores and keep them current. Venture-backed teams build the scalable thing instead, which is precisely why the unscalable thing is available.

The moat

What is actually defensible

Not the code. It was built in two days and can be rebuilt in two days.

The defensible assets are the 43-store directory with real brands, prices, and insider detail, and the Houston knowledge around it: that CERAWeek is suit week, that boots carry genuine formal standing at NRG in March, that the walk from a downtown garage in August is what decides your fabric. That knowledge decays, which is the point — it demands continuous work, and continuous work is what a funded generalist will not do for one city.

The strongest version is not software at all. Real relationships with M Penner and Hamilton Shirts — you send qualified buyers, they give your users something in return — cannot be replicated from a laptop in another state.

The uncomfortable part

Your moat is two days of AI research, and a competitor with the same tools can do two days of AI research. What they cannot shortcut is verifying it, earning trust in Houston, and having a stylist whose face people recognize. Which is why Kyla becoming a real person is not a branding nicety. It may be the actual defensibility.
